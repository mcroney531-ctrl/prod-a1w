The Core Finding

This is the floor, not the ceiling.

Out of the box, every user gets the same baseline: the AI coach, the standard features, a reasonable starting experience. What happens after that point is not fixed — the ceiling is set by what the user brings to it and how far they choose to take it. Nearly everything below is either evidence of that floor, or a response to how high the ceiling could go.

The limits are part of the promise, not a weakness: it should be clear about what it can't access, what it's inferring, and when the user needs to verify something in the app or with a person. That transparency makes the 'beyond the interface' experience feel useful rather than magical."

03

Wishlist

A few rough edges surfaced this week. Framed under the core thesis, these read less as broken promises and more as specific spots where the floor is still settling.

  • Memory across sessions — stated preferences don't appear to persist. A current limit on how high the floor sits today, not evidence the ceiling isn't real.
  • Daily prompt/tip — assumed a generic course-participant context rather than adjusting to the actual role. A sag in one specific spot, not a signal the whole product is unreliable — and the product team has separately flagged this feature as still in progress.
  • Let the daily tip ask, not assume — rather than assuming role and context upfront, a light clarifying question on first use could invite the user to self-customize — self-correcting through interaction instead of requiring a manual rebuild later.
  • "Quick actions" — capturing for later, to expand — something like: "I want to say X to this person — help me cater it to Y teammate," or "here's my current draft, adjust it for them." Still working out the right verb — not quite "draft," not quite "rewrite" — but the shape of it is a fast, targeted assist rather than a full compose-from-scratch. More to come here.
04

What This Means for How We Teach It

The current org model for rolling out a feature is straightforward: the feature is static, a walkthrough explains what it does, and that's sufficient. This product doesn't fit that model cleanly. Its actual ceiling depends entirely on what the user brings to it — a one-time walkthrough won't teach someone how to get long-term value out of something that rewards ongoing use.

The practical implication: learning content likely needs to shift from here's what this feature does to here's how you can make the most of it — teaching usage patterns and habits, not just surface functionality. You only get out what you put in, and that has to be taught deliberately. It won't be assumed correctly on its own.
